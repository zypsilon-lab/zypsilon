ZYpsilon · Projection lab

A free, interactive 3D laboratory for descriptive geometry. See how orthogonal projections (Monge), axonometry, perspective, shadows and sections are built: a projection plane, a solid, the projecting rays and the image that forms on the plane. All in your browser, nothing to install.

Open the lab

Opens in English, free and with no sign-up.

What you can do

  • Orthogonal projections (Monge): three projection planes (VP, HP, LP) with the solid in the first dihedral. The folding slider lays the planes down on the drawing sheet: front view, plan and side view with the ground line and projection lines.
  • Orthogonal and oblique axonometry: isometric; in advanced mode also dimetric and trimetric. Oblique: cavalier, cabinet, monometric, planometric, reduced planometric and military.
  • Perspective: frontal (one vanishing point), accidental (two) and three-point, with horizon line, ground line, principal point and vanishing points.
  • Cutting plane and sections: the solid is really cut: the removed part stays faintly visible, the section is coloured and in Monge it folds down to give the true shape with the classic arc construction.
  • Shadows: parallel rays (sun) or a point source (lamp): own shadow, cast shadow and a guided construction, one vertex at a time.
  • Intersection Lab: the interpenetration of two solids by the cutting-plane method: choose solids A and B, set axes and positions and follow the construction.
  • Drawing sheet and PDF: choose A4, A3 or A2, with measures in centimetres and the drawing at 1:1; download PNG or PDF with a link that reopens that very drawing.

How to use it

  1. Choose the projection type in the left panel.
  2. Drag to rotate the view, Shift + drag to pan, wheel or pinch to zoom. On touch screens one finger rotates, two fingers pan and zoom.
  3. Pick or measure the solid and switch on the cutting plane, shadows or measures when you need them.
  4. Lay the drawing out on the sheet and download it as PNG or PDF, or copy the link to reopen the scene.

Who it is for

Students and teachers of descriptive geometry, technical drawing and representation in art schools, technical schools and university courses in architecture, engineering and design. It also works on tablets and interactive whiteboards.

Frequently asked questions

Is ZYpsilon free?
Yes, for study, teaching and research, under the PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0 licence. Students, teachers, schools, universities and public research bodies can use it freely. Commercial use (for example in books, paid courses or ad-supported sites) needs a written licence: info@zypsilon.com.
Do I need to install anything?
No, it runs in the browser. If you prefer, download the repository from GitHub and open index.html, even offline.
Which languages are available?
Italian, English and Simplified Chinese. It opens in the language of your browser; the selector at the top of the panel changes it.
Does it collect personal data?
No accounts and nothing is sent to a server: scenes, PNG and PDF are produced on your device. Google Analytics statistics load only on the official site and only after you consent.
How do I cite it in a thesis or paper?
ZYpsilon is archived on Zenodo, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24405119, which always points to the latest version. Author: Baires Raffaelli.
